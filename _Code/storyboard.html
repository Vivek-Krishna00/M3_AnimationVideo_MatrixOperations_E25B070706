Storyboard: What does a matrix do?

Matrices and matrix operations · Vivek Krishna · E25B070706

By the end of this video, the viewer will be able to explain a matrix as a movement of the plane, and why addition, scaling and multiplication of matrices work the way they do.

Example throughout: A = [0 −1; 1 0] (quarter turn), B = [1 1; 0 1] (shear), v = (1, 2). Semicolons separate rows. Colours: e₁ green, e₂ red, v yellow, A blue, B purple.

1. Hook

Visual: one grid and letter F, two moves in opposite orders.

Narration: same moves, different pictures. Why does order matter?

Maths: none yet; the puzzle is BA against AB.

2. Concept introduction

Visual: 2 × 3 matrix, entry c₂₃ = 4, machine diagram, basic arrows.

Narration: sizes, entries, columns as landing spots, learning goal.

Maths: m × n takes n numbers in and gives m out.

3. Visual intuition

Visual: shear moves grid and v; outputs add tip to tail; output doubles.

Narration: same recipe, new arrows.

Maths: Bv = 1(1,0) + 2(1,1) = (3,2); Av = (−2,1); (A+B)v = (1,3); 2Bv = (6,4).

4. Worked example

Visual: columns of B pushed through A, then four entries highlighted.

Narration: row times column is bookkeeping for move after move.

Maths: AB = [0 −1; 1 1], all four sums shown.

5. Interpretation

Visual: the two final grids with their matrices; chained machines.

Narration: solves the puzzle and explains the size rule.

Maths: BA = [1 −1; 1 0]; ABv = (−2,3); BAv = (−1,1); (m×n)(n×p) = m×p.

6. Summary and credits

Visual: three takeaways, then closing frame.

Narration: columns are landing spots; sums and scales act on outputs; products are moves in order.
